# Commercial Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation and decision signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free-to-paid conversion | Local scanning may satisfy most users, leaving limited hosted demand. | Keep Community useful; interview active teams; meter requests for CI/history; build paid workflows only after repeated willingness-to-pay evidence. |
| Web3 budget volatility | Token and funding cycles can abruptly reduce software budgets. | Keep fixed costs low, use monthly plans for small teams, pursue annual Enterprise contracts cautiously, and avoid revenue assumptions tied to token prices. |
| Open-source monetization | Hosted competitors or self-operation may reduce paid conversion. | Monetize convenience, governance, support, and private deployment; preserve trusted portable formats; compete on workflow reliability rather than code restriction. |
| Hosted infrastructure cost | Compilation, scanning, storage, and CI concurrency may erode margins. | Measure cost per scan/project, set evidence-based quotas, isolate abusive workloads, use retention controls, and review contribution margin by plan. |
| Enterprise sales cycle | Procurement, security review, and deployment requirements delay revenue. | Do not depend on Enterprise for initial survival; standardize security evidence; qualify pilots; price onboarding/support obligations into annual contracts. |
| Arc adoption dependency | Narrow ecosystem adoption could limit the addressable customer base. | Serve concrete Arc workflows while retaining portable Solidity analysis and open report contracts; track Arc-specific demand without claiming guaranteed ecosystem growth. |
| Support burden | Security-adjacent findings can create high-expectation, expert support load. | Publish bounded claims and known limitations; tier response commitments; create triage playbooks; include dedicated support only in properly priced contracts. |
| Audit-tool confusion | Customers may treat findings or a clean scan as assurance equivalent to an audit. | Repeat “not an audit/formal verification” language in product, reports, contracts, and onboarding; require human review; never market corpus performance as universal correctness. |
| Pricing mismatch | Initial ranges may be too high for individuals or too low for team support costs. | Run willingness-to-pay tests, monitor plan-level costs and support hours, and treat all current ranges as hypotheses. |
| Billing/compliance readiness | Card or USDC subscriptions introduce tax, custody, sanctions, refund, and operational questions. | Use a payment-neutral billing abstraction; obtain appropriate review; launch USDC only after compliance and production validation. |
| Data privacy and tenant isolation | Hosted private repositories and findings are sensitive. | Minimize retained data, define deletion/retention, encrypt and isolate tenants, offer private runners/self-hosting only after security validation. |
| Detector trust and scope | False confidence or noisy findings can harm adoption. | Preserve deterministic tests and bounded benchmarks, disclose limitations, measure production feedback, and reinvest revenue in detector quality and Arc coverage. |

## Review cadence

Commercial risks should be reviewed monthly during hosted pilots and before each paid capability launch. A risk is not closed merely because its roadmap feature ships; closure requires production evidence and an accountable owner.
